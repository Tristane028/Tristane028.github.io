01 · Springer fibers · ongoing

A simple rank test predicts 209 of 210 closures. The one it misses is the singular component.

We computed which cells each cell closure meets for 116 configurations with two, three and four rows, up to n = 11. Then we looked for a rule that explains them. A rank condition does. None of the 1,144,312 relations breaks it, it gives exactly 2dim cells for every two-row closure, and for (3,2,2) it agrees with an exact Gröbner basis check everywhere except the one singular component.

Along the way we found that every cell can be read straight off its permutation, with nothing to solve. Cutting cells into smaller ones also turned up 64 relations the search had missed.

02 · Neural network Gaussian processes · MATH 216B

Deep ReLU networks forget the difference between inputs. erf and tanh hold on to some of it.

In the infinite-width limit, ReLU pushes the correlation between any two inputs toward 1 as depth grows. erf and tanh settle at a fixed point below 1 instead. I tested how well real finite-width networks follow those predictions.

With Prof. Alexander Cloninger · PyTorch, NumPy · Gaussian processes, theoretical ML
